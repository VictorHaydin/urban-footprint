Method & data

This site shows how much of each city's administrative territory is physically occupied by buildings, roads, railways, green space, water and other features. All numbers are computed reproducibly from OpenStreetMap data by a local processing pipeline; this page only displays precomputed results.

How the area breakdown is computed

  1. Source. A country extract from Geofabrik (daily OSM snapshot). The city is the area inside its OSM administrative boundary relation. The OSM data date is shown for every city.
  2. Classification. Every OSM way or multipolygon is assigned to at most one category / sub-category by tag rules (e.g. building=apartments → Buildings › Apartment buildings, landuse=forest → Green › Forests).
  3. Physical footprints. Polygons are used as mapped. Roads, railways, streams and runways are mapped as centre-lines, so they are widened ("buffered") to an estimated width: the width tag if present, otherwise lanes × lane width, otherwise a default per road type (e.g. residential street 6 m, footway 2 m, railway track 4.5 m). The share of area based on default widths is reported in the data-quality panel.
  4. No double counting. Categories are "painted" onto the map in priority order. Each one only receives the area not already taken by a higher-priority one: buildings → railway tracks → roads → parking → water → sports → green space → cemeteries → agriculture → industrial land → railway land → residential land → other land use. A building inside an industrial zone is therefore counted only as a building; the industrial category is the remaining (non-built, non-road…) land.
  5. Unclassified. Anything not covered by a classified OSM feature is reported as "Unclassified (no OSM data)". All categories together equal exactly 100% of the administrative area.

Reading the numbers

  • Buildings are actual building footprints (roof outlines), not residential districts. Buildings tagged only building=yes are kept as Unknown type; they are typed only when the same OSM object carries an explicit hint such as amenity=school or shop=*.
  • Bridges count as roads/railways (top-down view); tunnels and underground metro lines are excluded.
  • "Residential land" is the part of residential areas (landuse=residential) not covered by buildings, streets, parking, mapped lawns, playgrounds etc. — mostly courtyards, yards and unmapped open space between houses.
  • "Other mapped land" is land inside commercial, institutional or military land-use polygons (and airports, construction sites, garage cooperatives…) not covered by any more specific feature.
  • Results depend on how completely OSM mappers have covered each city; differences between cities can partly reflect mapping completeness rather than reality.

Map

At city scale the map shows anti-aliased coverage rasters (about 8 m resolution). When you zoom in, it switches to vector tiles that contain each individual counted piece. Click a piece to see its category, the OSM tag it was classified by, its exclusive area and a link to the OSM object.

Choose a second city under "Compare with" to see two cities side by side. Both maps always use the same ground scale (corrected for latitude, as Web Mercator enlarges places further north) and zoom together, so their sizes compare directly; each map can be panned independently to compare different parts of the cities. The sidebar then shows both cities category by category.
