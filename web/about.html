Method & data

This site shows how much of each city's administrative territory is physically occupied by buildings, roads, railways, green space, water, residential and industrial land and other features. All numbers are computed reproducibly from OpenStreetMap data by a local processing pipeline; the site only displays precomputed results.

Cities

Each city is the area inside its OSM administrative boundary: the city itself, not the wider urban hromada. All cities use the same OSM snapshot.

Territory: City, Hromada, Greater City

The "Territory" switch on the map and comparison pages sets which territory is measured:

  • City: the city's own administrative boundary.
  • Hromada: the whole urban territorial community (hromada) the city belongs to, including its villages and the land between them. Kyiv, a city with special status, has no separate hromada, so the city boundary is shown. Temporarily occupied cities also show the city boundary. Some hromadas (e.g. Kharkiv's) contain no other settlements, so the result equals the city.
  • Greater City: the city plus every settlement from which a typical car trip to the nearest built-up part of the city takes at most 10 minutes (free-flow, no traffic). Time is always measured to the city itself, never via other suburbs. The settlement's whole territory is added, whatever hromada or oblast it belongs to. Where OpenStreetMap maps a settlement only as a point, its outline is approximated from the built-up land around that point.

Every figure is computed separately for each mode, and everything outside the selected territory is hidden on the map. The list of Greater City settlements with drive times is shown in the map sidebar. The Hromada and Greater City modes are computed when a city is processed; until then the city boundary is shown.

How the area breakdown is computed

  1. Source. The Geofabrik extract for Ukraine (a daily OpenStreetMap snapshot), processed locally.
  2. Classification. Every OSM way or multipolygon is assigned to at most one category / sub-category by tag rules (e.g. building=apartments → Buildings › Apartment buildings, landuse=forest → Green › Forests).
  3. Physical footprints. Polygons are used as mapped. Roads, railways, streams and runways are mapped as centre-lines, so they are widened ("buffered") to an estimated width: the width tag if present, otherwise lanes × lane width, otherwise a default per road type (e.g. residential street 6 m, footway 2 m, railway track 4.5 m). The share of area based on default widths is reported in the data-quality panel. Mapped road surfaces are used as is, but a highway=* polygon only counts when it is explicitly marked area=yes. This guards against tagging errors such as the Dnipro Reservoir, which is tagged as a residential street in OSM.
  4. No double counting. Categories are "painted" onto the map in priority order. Each one only receives the area not already taken by a higher-priority one: buildings → railway tracks → roads → parking → water → sports → green space → cemeteries → agriculture → industrial land → railway land → residential land → other land use.
  5. Unclassified. Anything not covered by a classified OSM feature is reported as "Unclassified (no OSM data)". All categories together equal exactly 100% of the administrative area.

Categories

  • Buildings: actual building footprints (roof outlines), not residential districts. They are typed by building=* (houses, apartment buildings, commercial, industrial, public, other). Buildings tagged only building=yes are kept as Unknown type; they are typed only when the same OSM object carries an explicit hint such as amenity=school or shop=*.
  • Roads & streets and Railways: carriageways, pedestrian and cycle paths, tracks, tram lines and platforms. Bridges count (top-down view); tunnels and underground metro lines don't.
  • Parking, Water, Parks, forests & green space, Sports & recreation, Cemeteries, Agricultural land: mapped land cover and land use.
  • Residential land and Industrial land: only the land of these areas. The buildings standing on it are counted under Buildings, and roads, parking, lawns and playgrounds inside go to their own categories. What remains is mostly courtyards, yards and unmapped open space.
  • Other mapped land: commercial, institutional and military areas, airports, construction sites and garage cooperatives not covered by any more specific feature.
  • Unclassified: no OSM data. A large share usually means incomplete mapping.

Population

Population is shown on the map sidebar, in the "Population" breakdown of the comparison page and on the "Agglomerations" page. Each of them has a source (year) switch.

Sources

  • as of 1 January 2022 — State Statistics Service of Ukraine: de facto population (estimate) by oblast, raion, hromada and settlement, with KATOTTG codes.
  • 2001 census — All-Ukrainian census of 5 December 2001: de facto population of every settlement with its name, type, raion and council at the time, but no codes (compiled from the census regional tables on Pop-stat).

How population is computed

  • City — the row of the city itself. Hromada — the hromada total (for 2001 the sum of its settlements, as hromadas did not exist yet). Where there is no hromada (Kyiv, cities occupied before 2020), the population of the city is shown.
  • Greater City — the city plus every suburb. Settlements are matched to the table by the KATOTTG code from OpenStreetMap (katotth tag), otherwise by name and type in the surrounding hromadas, raions or oblast. Settlements without data are listed in the sidebar, so the total is a lower bound.
  • Density — population over the whole territory (including forests, water and fields), so it depends strongly on where boundaries are drawn.

2001 census: matching to present-day settlements

Census rows get the present-day KATOTTG code by name within the oblast. Candidates are Derzhstat settlements and OpenStreetMap points (current name and former old_name, which covers most renamings). Namesakes are told apart by the council at the time (the hromada its other villages went to), then by raion, then by the closest type; a separate pass handles spelling variants (є/е, і/и). A few renamings OSM does not record and settlements merged into cities since (Inhulets into Kryvyi Rih, Taromske into Dnipro) are set by hand. Where OSM has no codes (most of Crimea), the OSM point itself is used. 98.9% of the census population is matched; the city figure is the census figure for the city boundary at the time.

The "Agglomerations" page

  • Agglomeration centres are all cities of over 50,000 people in the 2001 census (101). Some of them are on this page only so far: only their OSM city boundary is used.
  • For each city the free-flow car drive time (OSM road network, typical speeds) is computed from any road inside the city to every settlement of Ukraine within 3 hours.
  • For the chosen threshold (city only, 10, 20, 30, 45 min, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5 or 3 h) cities are taken from the largest down: a city joins the agglomeration of another only if it is within the threshold and the other is more than twice its size. Cities of comparable size stay separate centres.
  • Every other settlement joins the centre it is reached from fastest, if within the threshold.
  • These two rules work independently. A city that joins a larger centre stops being a centre itself, and the settlements around it do not follow it: they join the nearest active centre. An agglomeration can therefore have outlying "islands". Example at 3 h: Mukachevo (86k) joins Lviv (717k, 176 min), because Lviv is more than twice its size and Uzhhorod (115k, 27 min) is not. Svaliava, Berehove, Irshava and Khust are closest to Mukachevo, but it is no longer a centre, and Uzhhorod is faster to reach from them than Lviv (from Khust Lviv is beyond 3 h altogether), so they go to Uzhhorod. Mukachevo is in the Lviv agglomeration, the settlements around it in the Uzhhorod one.
  • The agglomeration outline is a line through its outermost points (a concave hull). The points are its settlements and the boundaries of the cities in it (the centre and absorbed cities). They are triangulated, and a triangle is kept when no side is longer than 15 km. Detached parts (e.g. Mukachevo in the Lviv agglomeration at 3 h) are left out, only the contiguous part around the centre is outlined, and holes are not shown.
  • On the map, centre circles have an area proportional to the agglomeration population. Settlements over 50k are circles on the same scale, 10–50k bright dots, 1–10k paler dots, under 1k faint dots.
  • The road network is the intact, pre-war one: destroyed bridges and roads count as intact, closures of main roads are ignored, and roads cut in OSM along the front line are joined again. The Kakhovka dam road is not restored.

Derzhstat does not cover Crimea and Sevastopol, so Simferopol and Sevastopol have 2001 census data only. For territories occupied since 2014 the 2022 figures are estimates. Where the OSM city boundary differs from the official one, the population refers to the official city and the density may be over-estimated.

Using the site

Map

  • At city scale the map shows anti-aliased coverage rasters (tiled, down to about 6 m resolution). When you zoom in, it switches to vector tiles with each individual counted piece. Click a piece to see its category, the OSM tag it was classified by, its exclusive area and a link to the OSM object.
  • The sidebar lists every category with its share or area (switch between % and km²). Click a colour swatch to hide or show a category; expand a category to see its sub-categories. With Sub-categories as % of category (on by default), building types and other sub-categories add up to 100% of their category; switch it off to see them as a share of the whole city.
  • Two cities side by side: choose a city under "Compare with". Both maps always use the same ground scale (corrected for latitude, as Web Mercator enlarges places further north) and zoom together, so their sizes compare directly; each map can be panned on its own to look at different parts of the cities. The sidebar then shows both cities category by category. Choose "— No city —" to return to a single map.

Compare cities

  • Each bar shows a city's whole territory: either as 100% (shares) or to scale in km², so larger cities get longer bars. "Breakdown" switches to the sub-categories of one category, such as building types.
  • Click a category on a bar or in the legend to focus on it. Only that category is shown, cities are ranked by it, and the largest value spans the full width, so even small differences are visible. Click it again or ✕ to return.
  • Use the Cities filter to choose which cities to compare. The selection is kept in the page link. The data table below the chart is collapsed by default.

Data quality

The map page shows a data-quality panel for every city. It runs these checks:

  • whether the categories add up to the boundary area, and whether counted pieces overlap;
  • the share of unclassified land and of buildings with unknown type;
  • how much road and rail area relies on default widths;
  • OSM features that matched no rule;
  • suspicious features: implausibly large road, rail or parking polygons, which are usually OSM tagging errors.

Results depend on how completely OpenStreetMap volunteers have mapped each city. Differences between cities can partly reflect mapping completeness rather than reality. Check the unclassified share and the data-quality panel before drawing conclusions.
